## Quick Summary & How to Use This Checklist

Applying for commercial cyber insurance or completing an annual policy renewal has shifted from a brief questionnaire into a detailed technical evaluation. Insurance underwriters increasingly examine specific security controls—such as multi-factor authentication (MFA), immutable backups, closed remote management ports, and payment verification workflows—before quoting terms or binding coverage.

For small businesses without a dedicated IT department or in-house security officer, these questionnaires can be intimidating. Technical phrasing often obscures simple operational questions, and business owners frequently struggle to know what documentation or evidence an insurer expects to see.

This guide provides a practical, vendor-neutral framework to help small businesses:
1. **Understand common security questions** asked during cyber insurance applications.
2. **Identify technical and operational control gaps** before submitting an application.
3. **Assemble verifiable evidence and documentation** to streamline broker discussions.
4. **Avoid the serious legal and coverage consequences** of misrepresenting security controls on insurance forms.

## 1. Critical Distinctions: Readiness vs. Eligibility vs. Warranties vs. Coverage

Before reviewing individual controls, it is essential to understand the insurance terminology that governs applications, binders, and policies. Conflating these terms can lead to unrealistic expectations or severe coverage disputes.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                 THE INSURANCE TERMINOLOGY SPECTRUM                                |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. Security Readiness       ->  Operational controls are actively configured in your environment.|
|  2. Application Readiness    ->  You have verifiable evidence, logs, and documentation organized.  |
|  3. Underwriting Criteria    ->  Insurers evaluate your risk profile to set pricing and terms.    |
|  4. Subjectivities to Bind   ->  Mandatory preconditions that must be satisfied before binding.    |
|  5. Policy Warranties        ->  Contractual conditions requiring ongoing operational compliance. |
|  6. Policy Exclusions        ->  Explicit scenarios or events where coverage does not apply.       |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Security Readiness vs. Application Readiness
- **Security Readiness:** Your organization has functional defensive controls running in daily operations—for example, enforcing multi-factor authentication on all user mailboxes or keeping offsite backups.
- **Application Readiness:** Your organization has gathered the export summaries, administrative configuration screenshots, written policies, and drill records required to prove those controls exist without guessing.

### Underwriting Questions vs. Subjectivities (Preconditions to Bind)
- **Underwriting Questions:** Informational inquiries used by underwriters to assess risk levels, determine deductible amounts, and calculate premium rates. An unfavorable answer may increase your premium or limit certain coverages, but does not necessarily prevent an insurer from offering a quote.
- **Subjectivities (Preconditions):** Non-negotiable requirements specified by an underwriter that must be completed and documented before a policy will be officially bound. For example, an insurer may issue a conditional quote subject to the applicant closing an exposed remote access port within 14 days.

### Policy Warranties vs. Policy Exclusions
- **Policy Warranties / Conditions Precedent:** Binding contractual clauses wherein the policyholder explicitly warrants that specific safeguards (such as MFA on all remote connections or dual-authorization on wire transfers) will remain continuously active throughout the entire policy period.
- **Policy Exclusions:** Specific clauses detailing risks or circumstances that the policy does not cover under any circumstances (such as losses resulting from unpatched vulnerabilities explicitly excluded by endorsement, prior known incidents, or regulatory fines where prohibited by law).

## 2. Application Accuracy & Legal Considerations: Lessons from Travelers v. ICS

The most critical rule in cyber insurance applications is **absolute accuracy**. Treating an application as an aspirational checklist—checking "Yes" because you intend to configure a setting next month, or because a control is 80% deployed—can create serious legal and coverage consequences.

### The "All" Trap
Insurance applications frequently use absolute qualifiers:
- *"Is Multi-Factor Authentication required for **all** remote access?"*
- *"Is MFA enforced for **all** administrative or privileged accounts?"*
- *"Are **all** company endpoints protected by centralized antivirus or EDR?"*

If your organization has 25 employees and 24 use MFA, but one legacy server, an unmonitored administrative account, or an outsourced contractor portal bypasses secondary authentication, answering "Yes" without qualification is factually inaccurate. If an attack occurs through that unmanaged exception, forensic investigators will uncover the gap.

### A Cautionary Case Study: Travelers v. International Control Services (2022)
The legal risk of inaccurate application representations was demonstrated in *Travelers Property Casualty Company of America v. International Control Services, Inc.* (Case No. 2:22-cv-02145, U.S. District Court for the Central District of Illinois).

- **The Dispute:** Travelers filed a complaint for declaratory judgment and rescission of a cyber insurance policy issued to International Control Services (ICS), an electronics manufacturer, following a ransomware attack. Travelers asserted that in its policy application, ICS had answered that MFA was utilized for administrative or privileged access. Following the attack, forensic investigation revealed that MFA was only active on the perimeter firewall and was not protecting server administrative consoles.
- **The Allegation:** Travelers contended that had it known the true state of the company's MFA implementation, it would not have issued the policy under the same terms or premiums.
- **The Resolution:** In August 2022, the parties resolved the lawsuit by filing a joint stipulation in which they agreed that Travelers relied on incorrect statements made in the application when issuing the policy. The court entered an agreed order rescinding the policy as **void from inception** (*ab initio*) and dismissing the case with prejudice.

> **Key Takeaway for Small Businesses:** The *Travelers v. ICS* matter illustrates that insurers examine application answers during post-incident forensic investigations. If an application contains inaccurate statements regarding security controls, insurers can—and do—seek to void the policy entirely through rescission, leaving the business without coverage. If a control is only partially deployed, disclose the scope accurately to your insurance broker rather than checking "Yes."

## 3. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): What Underwriters Scrutinize

**MFA is a widely scrutinized control in cyber-insurance underwriting, especially for remote access, administrative accounts, and cloud services.** Because credential theft and credential stuffing remain frequent initial intrusion vectors, underwriters focus heavily on eliminating single-password access points.

For comprehensive implementation guidance, follow our [practical MFA guide for small businesses](/guides/mfa-for-small-businesses).

### Where Insurers Commonly Look for MFA
1. **Remote Network Access:** All Virtual Private Networks (VPNs), remote desktop connections, and browser-based remote access gateways used by employees, remote workers, or third-party IT contractors.
2. **Administrative & Privileged Accounts:** Every account with elevated permissions, including cloud tenant super administrators, domain controllers, firewall managers, and billing portals.
3. **Cloud Productivity & Email:** All user accounts in cloud environments like [Microsoft 365](/guides/microsoft-365-security-small-business) or [Google Workspace](/guides/google-workspace-security-small-business).
4. **Mission-Critical Business Systems:** Access to payroll software, ERP platforms, code repositories, and backup management consoles.

### Phishing-Resistant vs. App-Based Methods
Underwriting questionnaires increasingly distinguish between secondary authentication methods:
- **Phishing-Resistant Methods (Highest Standard):** FIDO2 hardware security keys (e.g., YubiKeys) and passkeys. These rely on origin-bound cryptographic validation that cannot be intercepted by adversary-in-the-middle reverse proxies.
- **App-Based Verification (Standard Baseline):** Mobile authenticator apps generating Time-Based One-Time Passwords (TOTP) or secure number-matching push notifications (e.g., Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator).
- **SMS & Voice Verification (Widely Deprecated):** Text message codes and phone calls are vulnerable to SIM swapping and SS7 interception. Some applications ask whether SMS is permitted or explicitly ask whether it has been disabled for privileged accounts.

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Tenant Export Report:** An exported CSV or summary report from Microsoft Entra ID or Google Admin console showing MFA enrollment status across 100% of active accounts.
- **Policy Configuration Screenshots:** Administrative screenshots of enforced Conditional Access policies or 2-Step Verification domain-wide rules.

## 4. Data Backups & Recovery Architecture: Common Questions

Ransomware attacks represent a significant portion of commercial cyber insurance losses. Underwriters scrutinize backup architectures to determine whether a policyholder can restore operations independently or whether they will be forced to negotiate extortion demands to avoid catastrophic downtime.

For detailed architecture rules, read our guide on [small business backup and ransomware protection](/guides/small-business-backup-ransomware-protection).

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                 THE 3-2-1 RANSOMWARE-RESILIENT BACKUP                             |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  3 Copies of Critical Data     ->  One primary production copy, plus two distinct backup copies.   |
|  2 Different Storage Media     ->  e.g., local network-attached storage and cloud object storage. |
|  1 Offsite / Immutable Copy   ->  Cryptographically locked (WORM) or air-gapped from the domain. |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Key Technical Questions Underwriters Evaluate
- **Segmentation & Credential Isolation:** Are backup systems joined to the primary Active Directory domain? If an attacker compromises an administrative account on a workstation, can they use those credentials to access and delete the backups? (Underwriters expect backup management to use dedicated, isolated credentials protected by independent MFA).
- **Immutability (Write-Once-Read-Many):** Does the cloud storage or local repository enforce object locking or retention policies that prevent files from being modified, overwritten, or deleted—even by an administrator—for a specified retention window (e.g., 30–90 days)?
- **Air-Gapping:** Does the organization maintain offline physical copies (such as rotated external hard drives disconnected when not in use) or logically air-gapped cloud repositories?

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Backup Job Configuration Summary:** Documentation detailing the schedule (daily/hourly), retention period, and data sources covered.
- **Immutability Verification:** A screenshot or provider confirmation showing that object lock or immutable retention settings are active on the backup storage bucket.

## 5. Backup Restoration Testing: Verifying Recovery Capability

Having backup software running is only half of the recovery equation. **Some applications ask whether backups are periodically tested for restoration.** In actual ransomware incidents, organizations regularly discover that unverified backups contain corrupted database snapshots, missing decryption keys, or unviable image files.

### What Insurers Look For
- **Frequency of Testing:** Some questionnaires inquire whether full or partial file restorations are conducted annually, semi-annually, or quarterly.
- **Scope of Test:** Did the test verify that individual files could be recovered, or was a full system/bare-metal restore performed on an isolated test machine?
- **Restoration Duration:** How long does it realistically take to pull down 500 GB or 2 TB of business records across your current internet connection?

### How to Run a Low-Disruption Restoration Drill
1. **Select a Test Target:** Pick a sample dataset containing representative business documents, accounting records, or a non-production virtual machine image.
2. **Restore to an Isolated Location:** Restore the data to an independent folder or test machine. Never restore directly over live production files.
3. **Verify Data Integrity:** Open several sample files (spreadsheets, databases) to confirm they are readable, complete, and uncorrupted.
4. **Record the Metrics:** Document the date, start time, completion time, total data volume restored, and who conducted the drill.

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Restoration Drill Sign-off Sheet:** A 1-page PDF record documenting the date of the last test, systems restored, duration, outcome, and the signature of the business owner or IT lead.

## 6. Perimeter Security: Internet-Exposed Remote Desktop (RDP)

**Internet-exposed RDP is a well-known security risk and is commonly addressed in ransomware guidance and insurance underwriting.** Remote Desktop Protocol (running on standard port 3389) allows users to access a computer desktop over the network. When left open to the open internet, it becomes a target for automated credential stuffing, brute-force bots, and remote code execution exploits.

### Outside-In Attack Surface Scans
**Some insurers use external attack-surface checks or scanning as part of underwriting.** Before issuing a formal quote or binding coverage, carriers or their technical risk partners may run non-intrusive scans against your company's registered public IP addresses and domain names. If their scanners detect open port 3389, exposed database ports, or vulnerable firewall management portals, the application may be delayed, quoted with restrictive endorsements, or declined outright.

### Recommended Perimeter Hygiene
- **Close Public RDP:** Ensure port 3389 is completely closed on all external firewall interfaces and internet routers.
- **Require VPN or Secure SASE Gateways:** If remote access is necessary, require employees to connect first through an encrypted VPN gateway enforcing MFA, or utilize modern identity-aware reverse proxies (such as Cloudflare Access or Microsoft Entra Application Proxy).
- **Audit Router Configuration:** Follow our [small business Wi-Fi and network security guide](/guides/small-business-wifi-network-security) to verify external router port forwarding rules.

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Perimeter Firewall Port Report:** An export or administrative screenshot showing no inbound port forwarding rules mapped to internal server management interfaces.

## 7. Funds Transfer Fraud & BEC: Payment Verification Controls

**Payment diversion and business email compromise are important risks for small businesses, so insurers may ask about verification controls for payment or bank-account changes.** 

When cybercriminals compromise a vendor's or employee's email account, they alter bank account numbers on legitimate invoices or send urgent requests claiming supplier wiring instructions have changed. If staff process the payment without secondary verification, funds are diverted to attacker-controlled accounts.

For a full breakdown of payment scams, read our guide on [business email compromise and payment fraud prevention](/guides/business-email-compromise-small-business-payment-fraud).

### The Out-of-Band Callback Procedure
To qualify for Funds Transfer Fraud (FTF) or Social Engineering endorsements, applications commonly inquire whether the organization enforces a mandatory **out-of-band verification procedure**:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                             OUT-OF-BAND PAYMENT VERIFICATION PROTOCOL                             |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. Incoming Request         ->  Supplier emails: "Our bank account details have changed."        |
|  2. Stop & Flag              ->  Finance staff flags any change to account numbers or routing.     |
|  3. Independent Contact      ->  Staff looks up the vendor's phone number on a prior trusted invoice|
|                                  (NEVER use the phone number listed in the suspicious email).     |
|  4. Verbal Confirmation      ->  Staff calls the verified contact to verbally confirm the change.  |
|  5. Dual Sign-off            ->  Two separate staff members approve any wire transfer >$5,000.    |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Written Payment Verification Policy (SOP):** A simple 1-page written procedure signed by company leadership mandating phone callbacks on trusted numbers for banking changes and dual-authorization for transfers above a set threshold (e.g., $5,000 or $10,000).

## 8. Endpoint Security: Antivirus and Endpoint Detection Capabilities

Every laptop, workstation, and server in your organization represents a potential point of initial access. Underwriters inspect endpoint defenses to understand how quickly an infection will be contained.

For workstation hardening steps, review our [small business laptop and work computer security guide](/guides/small-business-computer-laptop-security).

### Traditional Antivirus vs. Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR)
- **Traditional Antivirus (Signature-Based):** Scans files against known signatures of existing malware. Effective against commodity malware, but ineffective against zero-day exploits, memory-only attacks, or living-off-the-land techniques using native administrative utilities.
- **Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR):** Continuously monitors process behaviors, script executions, and network connections across endpoints. If suspicious activity occurs (such as PowerShell attempting to dump credential hashes), EDR flags or isolates the device automatically.

### What Insurers Ask
**Some insurers may ask about EDR or comparable endpoint detection capabilities, particularly for organizations with higher risk profiles, larger workforces, or significant revenues.** For smaller businesses (under 10–15 endpoints), built-in defenses like Microsoft Defender Antivirus with real-time cloud protection are often evaluated, but as workforce size increases, centralized management and behavioral monitoring become more common underwriting topics.

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Endpoint Management Console Export:** A summary report from your centralized antivirus, EDR, or mobile device management (MDM) console demonstrating that active real-time protection is deployed across all managed business computers.

## 9. Patch Management & Supported Software

Software vulnerabilities in operating systems and third-party tools are frequently leveraged by automated exploitation scripts. Underwriters evaluate patch management to assess whether an organization maintains basic hygiene or leaves known vulnerabilities open for months.

### Critical Patch Remediation Timelines
Some applications ask about specific patch management service-level targets:
- **Operating System Updates:** Are automatic security updates enabled for Windows and macOS devices?
- **Critical Vulnerability Timelines:** How quickly are critical vulnerabilities (e.g., severe CVEs flagged in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog) patched once a fix is released (e.g., within 14 to 30 days)?
- **Third-Party Applications:** Are widely targeted web browsers, PDF readers, and office applications kept updated?

### The Risk of Unsupported Operating Systems
**Unsupported or end-of-life systems may affect underwriting, depending on the insurer and application.** Running operating systems that no longer receive vendor security updates (such as Windows Server 2012, Windows 7, or legacy macOS builds) represents a significant underwriting concern. If legacy systems must remain active for specific machinery or software, underwriters frequently ask whether they are isolated on a segregated VLAN with zero outbound internet access.

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Device Operating System Inventory:** A simple inventory table or MDM report showing device names, current operating system versions, and automated update configurations.

## 10. Incident Response Preparedness: Planning for Disruption

When a security incident occurs, the speed and structure of the initial response directly determines the overall financial loss and forensic investigation duration. Underwriters evaluate incident preparedness to ensure that a minor event does not escalate into an unmanaged crisis.

For an operational blueprint, follow our guide on [how to create a small business incident response plan](/guides/small-business-incident-response-plan).

### Key Components Insurers Evaluate
1. **Designated Incident Roles:** Is there an identified individual (and a designated alternate) authorized to make operational and legal decisions during an incident?
2. **Offline Emergency Contacts:** Is a printed copy of critical contact numbers maintained offline, including external IT support, corporate legal counsel, and banking representatives?
3. **Insurance Breach Notification Line:** Does the incident response plan list your cyber insurance carrier's claims reporting number? (Many policies mandate that the insurer be notified immediately, and failing to notify the carrier before hiring external forensic consultants can jeopardize reimbursement under policy terms).

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Documented Incident Response Sheet:** A 1- to 2-page emergency action document identifying response leads, communication protocols, and carrier contact information.

## 11. Employee Awareness & Access Governance

Human error and deceptive communications remain frequent catalysts for account compromise. Insurers evaluate how businesses manage employee awareness and whether access is promptly revoked when staff leave.

### Security Awareness Training & Phishing Drills
- **Training Frequency:** Some applications ask whether all employees complete regular cybersecurity awareness training (annually or upon onboarding).
- **Phishing Simulations:** Larger or higher-risk applicants may be asked whether periodic simulated phishing campaigns are conducted to evaluate employee reporting habits. Follow our [phishing protection guide](/guides/small-business-phishing-protection) for educational baselines.

### Access Revocation & Timely Offboarding
Dormant accounts belonging to departed employees or external contractors provide attackers with silent entry points. Underwriters ask whether access is promptly de-provisioned across cloud platforms, email, and password vaults.
- Follow our structured [Employee Offboarding Security Checklist](/guides/employee-offboarding-security-checklist) to ensure that mailbox sync, session cookies, and third-party SaaS tokens are terminated on departure day.

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Training Records:** A completion roster or platform export confirming staff attendance at annual cybersecurity briefings.
- **Offboarding Checklist Sign-offs:** Sample records showing completed offboarding sign-offs for recent departures.

## 12. Public DNS & Domain Authentication

Email spoofing and domain impersonation directly facilitate invoice fraud. **Some insurers and security-scanning services may inspect public DNS records for email-authentication controls such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.**

For domain hardening rules, read our dedicated guide on [small business domain name and DNS security](/guides/small-business-domain-name-security).

### Email Authentication Protocols
- **Sender Policy Framework (SPF):** A public DNS TXT record specifying which mail servers are authorized to send email on behalf of your domain.
- **DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM):** Cryptographic signatures embedded in outgoing message headers that verify email content was not altered in transit.
- **Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC):** A DNS record instructing recipient mail servers how to handle messages that fail SPF or DKIM checks (e.g., monitor, quarantine, or reject).

### Evidence to Prepare
- **Public DNS Record Exports:** Verification from your domain registrar or DNS management portal showing published SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records.

## 13. The 4-Week Pre-Application Preparation Sequence

Do not wait until the day an application is due to begin assessing security controls. Following a structured 4-week timeline allows your team to identify gaps, formalize configurations, and assemble a defensible evidence folder before meeting with an insurance broker.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                           4-WEEK CYBER INSURANCE PREPARATION TIMELINE                            |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  WEEK 1: Internal Control Audit                                                                   |
|  - Audit MFA status across all cloud email, remote access, and administrative accounts.           |
|  - Verify that Remote Desktop (port 3389) is closed to the public internet.                       |
|  - Confirm backup schedules, immutability settings, and credential separation.                   |
|                                                                                                   |
|  WEEK 2: Evidence Gathering & Documentation                                                       |
|  - Export administrative MFA configuration summaries and user enrollment reports.                 |
|  - Draft or update a 1-page written dual-authorization SOP for payments and bank account changes.  |
|  - Review endpoint security console reports to verify active protection on all computers.         |
|                                                                                                   |
|  WEEK 3: Operational Drills & Policy Finalization                                                 |
|  - Conduct a small, documented backup restoration drill and sign the drill log sheet.             |
|  - Review the written incident response sheet and confirm emergency contacts are current.         |
|  - Address any identified unsupported operating systems or decommission inactive accounts.        |
|                                                                                                   |
|  WEEK 4: Evidence Binder Assembly & Broker Review                                                 |
|  - Assemble all configuration screenshots, drill logs, and written SOPs into an evidence binder.  |
|  - Review application questions carefully with your insurance broker.                             |
|  - Disclose any partial implementations or planned upgrades transparently.                        |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 14. Ten Questions Small Businesses Should Ask Their Insurance Broker

When purchasing or renewing a cyber insurance policy, do not focus solely on the top-line coverage limit or premium cost. Ask your licensed insurance broker these ten essential questions to understand policy boundaries:

1. **Are there ransomware sublimits or co-insurance requirements?**  
   *Does a \$1,000,000 policy cap ransomware extortion or business interruption payouts at \$250,000?*
2. **What are the retention (deductible) amounts and waiting periods?**  
   *How much must the business pay out-of-pocket per incident, and how many hours of downtime must elapse before business interruption coverage begins?*
3. **Do any application answers become express policy warranties?**  
   *Are specific controls (e.g., MFA on all remote access) written into the policy as conditions precedent to coverage?*
4. **Is there an approved panel of incident response and forensic vendors?**  
   *Must we use the carrier's pre-approved forensic firms, breach coaches, and legal counsel, or can we choose our own?*
5. **What are the exact claim notification timing requirements?**  
   *Within how many hours or days must an incident be reported to the carrier to prevent coverage disputes?*
6. **Does the policy include Funds Transfer Fraud (FTF) and Social Engineering coverage?**  
   *Are invoice manipulation, payment diversion, and fraudulent wire transfers covered, and under what verification conditions?*
7. **Are unpatched vulnerabilities or end-of-life systems excluded?**  
   *Does the policy contain endorsements that exclude losses originating from known vulnerabilities or unsupported operating systems?*
8. **Does coverage extend to cloud service provider outages and SaaS data?**  
   *Are business interruption losses covered if Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, or your primary cloud hosting provider suffers a major outage?*
9. **Is regulatory fine and privacy liability defense included?**  
   *Does the policy cover legal defense and regulatory investigations relating to privacy or data protection regulations?*
10. **What risk-management or proactive scanning services does the carrier provide?**  
    *Does the policy bundle external vulnerability monitoring, security awareness training discounts, or pre-breach consultation services?*
