## Recommended order

1. **Business email compromise and payment verification** — the phishing guide introduces impersonation; a focused guide can give finance and supplier workflows.
2. **Software updates and patch management** — turn the checklist's update advice into an ownership and prioritization routine.
3. **Endpoint security for small businesses** — cover device coverage, built-in controls, support responsibilities, and when managed tools are appropriate.
4. **Secure Microsoft 365 accounts** — admin roles, MFA, recovery, mailbox rules, and data sharing, verified against current Microsoft documentation before publication.
5. **Secure Google Workspace accounts** — parallel audience need, but keep the guide product-specific and independently researched.
6. **Small-business risk assessment and asset inventory** — build on the checklist and connect systems, owners, and business impact.
7. **Practical employee cybersecurity training** — give small teams repeatable reporting and verification habits without duplicating the phishing guide.
8. **Wi-Fi security for a small office** — router administration, guest access, updates, and remote administration basics.
9. **Small-business data handling and retention** — clarify where sensitive information lives, who needs access, and how it should be removed.
10. **Secure remote work for a small team** — connect device, account, and network practices into a usable small-team routine.

Additional future gaps include secure remote work, SaaS access reviews, business-device offboarding, and securing domain/hosting accounts. Business email compromise is already mentioned in the phishing guide; publish it as a separate guide only if it has a sufficiently distinct payment-fraud intent.

These are planned search-intent gaps, not a commitment to publish every topic. Before drafting, search the registry and existing site pages for overlap, confirm the intended reader question, and identify trustworthy sources. Vendor-specific setup guides require a fresh review because settings and product interfaces change.
